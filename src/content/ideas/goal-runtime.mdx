Most agents work on tasks: do this thing, tell me when it's done. A lot of what I care about isn't shaped like that, though. "Keep checkout fast." "Make sure every big customer has a renewal plan a couple of months out." Nobody ever finishes those. You hold onto them, notice when they slip and figure out what to do.

So what if the goal itself was the thing that sticks around? You'd write down what should be true, what must never happen along the way, how you'd know it's true and who gets to make the calls. That would outlast any one model, tool or session. Some goals end once they're reached. Others, like keeping checkout fast, keep running and reopen whenever things drift.

<GoalLoop />

When something changes, it compares the world to the goal. If it's seen the situation before, it does what worked last time. If not, it explores.

<Futures />

I like the idea of a few planners throwing out options, including "do nothing yet" and "go find out more first", and then judging all of them against the same evidence. Anything that breaks a hard rule is out. When there's a real tradeoff, the person who owns it makes the call.

The other piece is being honest about what it knows. A tool call that came back successful isn't proof. It should go check what happened, and when it can't tell, say so.

<VerdictTimeline />

Every condition ends up satisfied, violated or unknown, and I think unknown is the one that matters most. Most systems treat no news as good news.

Over time it would collect what worked and what didn't and turn that into better rules and procedures, with a person signing off before anything gets more authority. The models wouldn't have to get any smarter for the whole thing to get better at acting.

It pairs with my other idea, an [agent harness built for apps](/ideas/app-agent-harness/). The harness runs the jobs, and the goal runtime decides what's worth doing and checks whether it worked.
